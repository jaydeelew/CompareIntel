## 📌 Exercise 1: Calculus Foundations

Given the function:  
$$ f(x) = x^2 + 2x + 1 $$

We are to compute:

1. The ***derivative*** $ f'(x) $  
2. The ***integral*** $ \int f(x) \, dx $

### ✅ Solution

The derivative is obtained using the power rule:  
$$
f'(x) = \frac{d}{dx}(x^2) + \frac{d}{dx}(2x) + \frac{d}{dx}(1) = 2x + 2
$$

The indefinite integral is:  
$$
\int f(x) \, dx = \int (x^2 + 2x + 1) \, dx = \frac{x^3}{3} + x^2 + x + C
$$  
where $ C $ is the **constant of integration** — a crucial reminder that antiderivatives are families of functions!
